**Overview**
A day trip from Naples to Sorrento can be comfortably done by train, ferry, or bus. Given that you’ll be traveling with elderly companions and wish to avoid anything too strenuous, the **Campania Express train** and the **hydrofoil ferry** stand out as the best options: both are relatively fast, comfortable, and require minimal walking or transfers. Below is a breakdown of each mode of transport, followed by a sample itinerary and practical tips.

---

## 1. Transportation Options

### A. Campania Express Train

* **Departure Point in Naples:**

  * Trains depart from **Napoli Porta Nolana** (lower level of Napoli Centrale station).
  * The Circumvesuviana platforms are accessed via stairs; there is no elevator to the lower-level platforms, so some assistance may be needed for those with mobility issues. ([sorrentoinsider.com][1], [positano.com][2])

* **Schedule & Duration:**

  * Four daily departures: **08:22**, **11:22**, **14:22**, **17:22** from Napoli Porta Nolana.
  * They stop only at a few stations (Ercolano Scavi, Pompei Scavi, etc.) before reaching Sorrento.
  * **Travel time:** \~45 minutes. ([positano.com][2])

* **Return Trains (Sorrento → Naples):**

  * Departures at **10:20**, **13:20**, **16:20**, and **19:20** from Sorrento, arriving back in Naples roughly 45 minutes later. ([booking.sorrentoinsider.com][3])

* **Cost & Comfort:**

  * One-way ticket: **€7 – €8** (Campania Express) or **€4 – €5** (regular Circumvesuviana) ([sorrentoinsider.com][1], [positano.com][2])
  * **Campania Express** trains offer guaranteed seating, air conditioning, and luggage storage—ideal for a comfortable ride. ([positano.com][2])

* **Accessibility Notes:**

  * While Sorrento station is accessible (elevator available), Napoli Porta Nolana has no elevator to the platforms and can be challenging if stairs are an issue.
  * If stairs are a major concern, consider the ferry (see below).

---

### B. Hydrofoil Ferry

* **Departure Point in Naples:**

  * **Molo Beverello** harbor (near Municipio metro stop).
  * Easily reached by taxi or a short metro ride to Municipio (Line 1). ([naplesbayferry.com][4], [sorrentoreview.com][5])

* **Schedule & Duration (April – October 2025):**

  * Multiple daily departures, for example:

    * **09:00** (Alilauro, 40 minutes)
    * **10:00** (NLG, 40 minutes)
    * **11:00**, **13:00**, **15:05**, **17:15**, **18:25**, etc.
  * **Travel time:**  \~ 40 – 45 minutes. ([naplesbayferry.com][4], [sorrentoreview.com][5])

* **Return Ferries (Sorrento → Naples):**

  * Departures throughout the afternoon/evening: e.g., **14:00**, **16:25**, **17:00**, **19:30** (seasonal variations apply).
  * Check the same booking platform used for outbound tickets. ([sorrentoreview.com][5])

* **Cost & Comfort:**

  * **One-way fare:** \~€15 – €18 per adult (varies by company and booking method). ([sorrentoreview.com][5], [aboutsorrento.com][6])
  * Hydrofoils are fast, well-maintained, and offer fantastic views of the Bay of Naples.
  * Boarding is relatively level, but you will need to walk on the dock. Assistance can often be requested at the ticket office or from crew if needed.

* **Accessibility Notes:**

  * All hydrofoils and ferries are wheelchair-accessible, but embarkation/disembarkation involves walking a short gangway. It’s recommended to call the company in advance to arrange assistance if required. ([aboutsorrento.com][6])

---

### C. SITA Bus

* **Departure Point in Naples:**

  * **Napoli Piazza Garibaldi** (outside Naples Centrale) or **Porta Nolana**.
  * Buses are less frequent than trains/ferries—check schedules ahead of time. ([hotbot.com][7])

* **Schedule & Duration:**

  * Buses take approximatively **1 hour 30 minutes** to **1 hour 45 minutes**, depending on traffic. ([hotbot.com][7])
  * Frequency varies; often one bus every 60 – 90 minutes.

* **Cost & Comfort:**

  * One-way fare: \~€3.80.
  * Air-conditioned coaches, but the ride can be bumpy on winding roads.
  * Not recommended if any mobility challenges exist, due to potential delays and stops.

* **Accessibility Notes:**

  * Boarding typically involves climbing 2–3 steps.
  * Space for luggage under the bus; minimal standing room, so best to arrive early to secure seats.

## 3. Detailed Recommendations & Tips

### A. Train (Campania Express)

1. **Buying Tickets:**

   * Purchase at ticket windows or automatic machines in **Napoli Centrale**.
   * No online sales—buy at least 15 minutes before departure. ([sorrentoinsider.com][1])

2. **Boarding Assistance:**

   * Since there is no elevator to Circumvesuviana platforms, ask station staff or a companion to help with luggage and boarding.
   * Once on board, seating is guaranteed; there is space for luggage.

3. **Comfort:**

   * Air conditioning ensures a comfortable ride, especially in summer.
   * Trains can sometimes be busy; aim for the 08:22 AM departure to avoid the mid-day crowds. ([positano.com][2])

---

### B. Ferry (Hydrofoil)

1. **Ticket Purchase:**

   * **Online booking** (e.g., via ferryhopper.com) is recommended to guarantee a seat—especially in high season (April–October).
   * Walk-up tickets available at Molo Beverello, but may sell out.

2. **Boarding:**

   * Ferries depart from **Pier 1 or Pier 3** at Molo Beverello—follow signage.
   * Accessible walkways; if any mobility aid is needed (e.g., wheelchair), contact the ferry company 24 hours in advance. ([aboutsorrento.com][6])

3. **On-board Comfort:**

   * Enclosed cabins with seating; sometimes there is an open-air deck (weather-dependent).
   * Restrooms available on board.

---

### C. Bus (SITA)

1. **Ticket Purchase:**

   * Buy in advance from tabaccherie (tobacconists) or bar/tabacchi near the departure point.
   * No on-board sales—validate the ticket once on board. ([europeandestinations.com][8])

2. **Boarding Point Details:**

   * Buses depart from **Piazza Garibaldi** (right outside Napoli Centrale) or **Porta Nolana**.
   * Look for “Sorrento – Amalfi – Positano” signage.

3. **Considerations for Elderly Travelers:**

   * The road is winding; some may experience discomfort.
   * Seats are cushioned; restrooms are not available.
   * Better to choose the 1st departure (around 08:00 AM) to avoid rush-hour traffic.

## 4. Sorrento: Easy, Senior-Friendly Activities

1. **Historic Center (Piazza Tasso & Corso Italia)**

   * Flat, pedestrianized areas with benches.
   * Plenty of cafés for rest breaks and people-watching.

2. **Villa Comunale Public Gardens**

   * Entrance is from a gentle paved ramp off Piazza Tasso; minimal stairs.
   * Benches overlooking the Bay; ideal for sitting and enjoying the view.

3. **Limoncello Tastings & Light Shopping**

   * Numerous small shops near Piazza Tasso offer tastings and seated areas.
   * No steep staircases; most are ground-floor or have a single step.

4. **Marina Grande (Optional)**

   * A small fishing village with flat promenades along the water.
   * Accessed via a short taxi ride (recommended over walking downhill).

5. **Accessible Restrooms & Amenities**

   * Public restrooms available in Piazza Tasso and outside the train station (both are wheelchair-accessible).

## 5. Practical Tips

* **Check Schedules in Advance:**

  * **Ferry** timetables change week to week; double-check within 2–3 days of travel. ([naplesbayferry.com][4], [sorrentoreview.com][5])
  * **Trains** generally run every 3–4 hours for Campania Express and every 30 minutes for Circumvesuviana.
  * **Buses** have less reliable schedules—plan extra time in case of delays.

* **Luggage Considerations:**

  * All transports allow at least one small suitcase per person.
  * Ferries charge \~€2 per additional piece; train and bus have no extra luggage fees.

* **Mobility & Assistance:**

  * At Naples Centrale and Porta Nolana, platform access is via stairs—arrive early to request assistance.
  * On ferries, accessible gangways available upon request; contact Alilauro or NLG in advance. ([aboutsorrento.com][6])

* **Seasonal Variations:**

  * From April – October, ferry frequency increases; off-season (November – March) is more limited. ([naplesbayferry.com][4], [aboutsorrento.com][6])
  * Campania Express runs year-round on its fixed schedule; Circumvesuviana runs daily from early morning until late evening.

* **Weather Considerations:**

  * If seas are rough, ferries may be suspended; always have a train or bus “backup plan.” ([naplesbayferry.com][4], [aboutsorrento.com][6])
  * In winter, trains are more reliable than maritime options.

### Summary

* **Recommended for Comfort & Ease:**

  * **Campania Express train** (08:22 departure → Sorrento by 09:05; return 16:20 departure → back in Naples by 17:05).
  * **09:00 AM hydrofoil** from Molo Beverello → Sorrento by 09:45; return 17:15 PM hydrofoil → back in Naples by 18:00.

* **Best for Scenic Views:**

* **Least Recommended (but viable):**

  * **SITA bus**, due to winding roads, longer travel time, and potential mobility challenges.

With these options and the outlined itinerary, you can enjoy a relaxed, scenic day trip to Sorrento that accommodates elderly travelers without any strenuous activity. Have a wonderful journey!
